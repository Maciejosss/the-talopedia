The **Age of the Kami** (神代, *Kamiyo*) is the period of [[kaiseido]] mythology from the creation of the world to the accession of the first sovereign of the human line, as recorded in the *Sōtenki* and the *Nichirin Shoki*. Its narrative accounts for the inclination of the heavens and the transfer of the earthly realm to the descendants of the sun kami Amaterasu. It is also the origin of the three imperial regalia: the mirror, the sword Kusanagi, and the seal of the land. Court calendricists of the [[hokyo-era|Hōkyō era]] dated the descent of Amaterasu’s grandson onto [[mount-soten|Mount Sōten]] to 2399 BCE and the conclusion of the age to the late 2nd century CE. Whether any part of the narrative corresponds to an archaeological culture has been disputed since the 1890s.

## Sources

Completed in 698, the *Sōtenki* (蒼天記, “Record of the Blue Heaven”) presents the myths as a single continuous narrative. The *Nichirin Shoki* (日輪書紀, “Chronicle of Nichirin”), completed in 731, arranges them as court history and follows most episodes with variant accounts introduced by the phrase “another record says.” Both were commissioned by the emperors of the [[mihashira-period|Mihashira period]] after the promulgation of the Hōkyō Code in 674. Neither has survived to the present in its original form; the extant texts descend from the recension prepared after the [[shogen-restoration|Shōgen Restoration]] of 890, which struck from the regnal lists the eleven emperors who had reigned since circa 780, known thereafter as the Eleven.

## Narrative

### Creation

In the *Sōtenki*, the first kami came into being in Sōten (蒼天, “Blue Heaven”), the High Plain of Heaven, which the chronicles place above the mountain of the same name. The last of their generations, Izanagi (伊邪那岐) and Izanami (伊邪那美), were commanded to give form to the land. They raised the Amenomihashira (天御柱, “Heavenly August Pillar”) as the axis between heaven and earth and circled it before uniting. Their offspring were the islands and coasts of [[nichirin]] and the earthly kami of its doorways, harvests, and river crossings.

Izanami died giving birth to the fire kami Kagatsuchi (迦具土). Izanagi followed her down the Yomotsu Hirasaka (黄泉比良坂), the slope to the land of the dead, but by the light of a burning comb, he saw her body in decay and fled as Izanami sent the dead after him. When he sealed the slope with a boulder, she vowed from the other side to take a thousand of the living each day, and he answered that he would cause fifteen hundred to be born. To cleanse himself of the pollution of the land of the dead, Izanagi purified himself in the sea. From the water he washed from his face were born Amaterasu (天照), the kami of the sun, and Susanoo (須佐之男), the kami of storms.

### Amaterasu and Susanoo

Amaterasu was given Sōten to govern and Susanoo the sea. Susanoo refused his charge and wept for his mother in the land of the dead until the seas dried, leading to his banishment by Izanagi. He ascended to Sōten to take leave of Amaterasu, who met him armed in the belief that he had come to seize it. To prove his intent, the two swore an oath and produced kami from each other’s possessions, and among those born from Amaterasu’s beads was Ame-no-Oshihomimi (天忍穂耳), the father of Ninigi. Claiming the oath as his victory, Susanoo laid waste to heaven. He broke the dikes of Amaterasu’s rice fields, and in the course of his rampage, he struck the Amenomihashira and left it leaning.

When a weaving maiden of heaven was killed in Susanoo’s rampage, Amaterasu withdrew into the Rock Cave of Heaven (天岩戸, *Ama-no-Iwato*) and deprived the world of light. The other kami hung a forged mirror at its mouth, and Ame-no-Uzume (天鈿女) danced before it until the assembly roared with laughter. Amaterasu looked out to learn the cause, saw her own light returned to her in the mirror, and was drawn out of the cave. Susanoo was expelled from heaven.

Susanoo descended to the northern coast, where an elderly couple had lost seven of their eight daughters to Yamata-no-Orochi (八岐大蛇), an eight-headed white serpent that descended from the north each winter. In exchange for the hand of the last daughter, Kushinada-hime (櫛名田比売), he intoxicated the serpent with rice wine and killed it. In its middle tail, he found the sword Kusanagi (草薙剣), which he gave to Amaterasu in reconciliation.

### Kuniyuzuri

*Main article:* [[kuniyuzuri]]

The land was governed by the earthly kami under Ōkuninushi (大国主), a descendant of Susanoo and Kushinada-hime. Amaterasu claimed it as the inheritance of her descendants. Her first envoys took the side of Ōkuninushi and did not return. The last, Takemikazuchi (建御雷) and Futsunushi (経津主), defeated one of his sons in a trial of strength. Ōkuninushi ceded the realm and surrendered the seal of the land.

### Descent onto Sōten

*Main article:* [[descent-onto-soten|Descent onto Sōten]]

Amaterasu sent her grandson, Ninigi (瓊瓊杵), down from heaven with the mirror, Kusanagi, and the seal, and charged him to keep the mirror as he would keep her. At Ame-no-Yachimata (天八衢), the crossroads of heaven, the procession was met by the earthly kami Sarutahiko (猿田彦). When Ame-no-Uzume confronted him, he agreed to guide it down, and Ninigi descended onto Mount Sōten.

### The Sōten generations

Ninigi married Konohanasakuya-hime (木花咲耶姫), the kami of the mountain. When he doubted that a child conceived in a single night was his, she gave birth on the burning summit, and the survival of her sons proved them divine. The younger, Hoori (火遠理), lost the fishhook of his elder brother, Hoderi (火照), and followed it into the sea to Ryūgū (竜宮), the palace of the sea king Wadatsumi (綿津見), whose daughter Toyotama-hime (豊玉姫) he married. He returned with the jewels of the tides, with which he subdued Hoderi, and Hoderi’s descendants served his line thereafter. The chronicles trace Hoori’s line through the sovereigns of Sōten, several of whom are credited with reigns of more than three centuries, to the heir who brought the mirror down from the mountain and was accepted by the chiefs of the land as the first sovereign of the human line.

### Variants

The *Nichirin Shoki* departs from the *Sōtenki* at several points.

- Izanagi and Izanami conceive Amaterasu and Susanoo together before Izanami’s death, and the descent to the land of the dead is given only as a variant.
- It gives the inclination of the Amenomihashira as half a right angle, and attributes the severity of [[avium]]'s seasons to it.
- It describes Yamata-no-Orochi’s breath as frost and its coming as the onset of winter.
- It adds that some of the earthly kami refused the Kuniyuzuri and withdrew east beyond the [[chohaku-mountains|Chōhaku Mountains]].

## Chronology

No event in heaven is dated. The court calendricists who prepared the chronology of the *Nichirin Shoki* held the [[raising-of-the-asuka-amenomihashira]] in 662 to be a repetition of the Descent onto Sōten. They placed the Descent exactly fifty-one sexagenary cycles earlier, in 2399 BCE, and distributed the reigns of the Sōten generations across the following centuries to the accession of the first human sovereign. The *Sōtenki* gives no dates.

## Historicity

Kaiseido scholarship and the school of continuity hold that the Sōten generations preserve an oral memory of the Late Nawame and the Hinode period. They identify the earthly kami with the stone circles and coastal settlements of the Late Nawame, and read the birth of Hoderi and Hoori on the burning summit as the record of an eruption of Mount Sōten. The school of replacement regards the age as a composition of the Mihashira court. It notes that no polity resembling the realm of the Sōten generations is attested before the ring tumuli of the Okutsuki period, and that Hezhouan records of the 2nd century describe more than a hundred chiefdoms and no sovereign.

## Religious significance

Izanagi’s purification after his return from the land of the dead is the model for the rite of purification at the center of Kaiseido practice. The chronicles derive the name Nichirin (日輪, “solar halo”) from the ring of light Amaterasu saw in the mirror at the Rock Cave of Heaven. Kaiseido tradition identifies the cave with Amaterasu’s Rest, a lava tube in [[k-sh|Kōshū]] Prefecture in [[tohanto]]. The shrine network of Sōtenroku Ward in Shinkeisei, at the foot of Mount Sōten, is dedicated to Konohanasakuya-hime and the kami of the Descent, as is the nearby Konohanasakuya Goshin’en, the home ground of [[nichirin-league-one]] football club [[kannushi]].

## Political usage

The emperors of the Mihashira period grounded their rule in the events of the age. The Raising of the Asuka Amenomihashira was staged as the raising of the pillar of creation. The court identified the earthly kami who refused the Kuniyuzuri with the Tohantan people, and cited their refusal to justify the Pacification of Tohanto.

From the [[sundering-of-the-regalia]] in 1185, Kusanagi fell into the possession of the Hayakawa Shogunate, and the warrior houses took Susanoo, who had drawn it, as their patron. The shogunates continued to observe the rites of the kami while denying the emperors’ descent from Amaterasu, retrospectively and collectively renaming the imperial era to the Nisekōtei (偽皇帝, “false emperors”) era. After the execution of Emperor Kasuga in 1868, the Bakufu retained the age in the national curriculum, which presents the descendants of Ninigi as the ancestors of the Nichirian people, making no reference to the imperial line.
