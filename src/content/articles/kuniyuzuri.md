The **Kuniyuzuri** (国譲り, “transfer of the land”) is the episode of the [[age-of-the-kami]] in which Ōkuninushi, ruler of the earthly kami, ceded the land to the descendants of the sun kami Amaterasu. It is recorded in the *Sōtenki* and the *Nichirin Shoki* and precedes the [[descent-onto-s-ten|Descent onto Sōten]].

## Narrative

Amaterasu declared from Sōten that the land, governed by Ōkuninushi (大国主) from his palace on the shore of Inasa, should pass to her descendants. Her first two envoys took Ōkuninushi’s side. Ame-no-Hohi (天穂日) sent no report for three years, while Ame-no-Wakahiko (天若日子) married Ōkuninushi’s daughter and was killed by an arrow cast down from heaven. The third embassy was entrusted to Takemikazuchi (建御雷) and Futsunushi (経津主), who descended onto the shore of Inasa and put Amaterasu’s demand to Ōkuninushi. He referred them to his sons. Kotoshironushi (事代主) consented to the transfer. Takeminakata (建御名方) challenged Takemikazuchi to a trial of strength, lost, and fled.

Ōkuninushi then ceded the land and surrendered the seal under which he had assigned the earthly kami their places. In return, he was given a dwelling whose pillars reached the High Plain of Heaven, and he retired to govern the unseen affairs (幽事, *kakuregoto*), leaving the visible affairs (顕事, *arawanigoto*) to Amaterasu’s line. The earthly kami of doorways, harvests, and river crossings kept their places under the new order.

In a variant recorded in the *Nichirin Shoki*, Takeminakata never submitted. He withdrew east beyond the [[chohaku-mountains|Chōhaku Mountains]] with the earthly kami who refused the cession, and swore to remain there for as long as the seal stayed with Amaterasu’s line. In this account, the land beyond the Chōhaku was never transferred. Commentators at the [[mihashira-period|Mihashira]] court identified the Tohantan people as the descendants of Takeminakata’s followers, and decrees issued during the [[pacification-of-tohanto]] describe its campaigns as the completion of the transfer. The reports of the Mihashira governors in Tohanto record that the Tohantans regarded their kami as the first rulers of the land, who had ceded nothing to the heavenly line, and that they refused the rites of the imperial cult on those grounds.

## Re-enactments

The kings who survived the [[raising-of-the-asuka-amenomihashira]] in 662 swore an oath to the throne on the model of the Kuniyuzuri, ceding their lands while retaining the rites of their clan kami. After the [[sundering-of-the-regalia]] in 1185, writers in the service of the [[hayakawa-shogunate]] applied the same terms to the court. The emperor retained the unseen affairs and the mirror, and the shogun governed the visible realm.
