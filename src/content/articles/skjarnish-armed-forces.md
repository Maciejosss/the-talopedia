The **Skjarnish Armed Forces** (Skjarnish: Krigsmakten, 'War Force') is the national military of [[skjarnland]]. It consists of 3 service branches: the Skjarnish Army, the Skjarnish Navy, and the Skjarnish Air Force. Despite the end of the Great War and Skjarnland's defeat in the Northern Front, setting the country's foreign policy as a neutral and isolationist power, the nation maintains a large active military as a form of deterrence and source of national pride. The military, including the Army in particular, is an old and historically well-respected institution in Skjarnland, its traditions dating back to Boehusite and Skjarnian militaries of the 10th-14th centuries and reaching its zenith in the 17th-18th centuries during the Great Power era, holding territory in modern day [[karjania]] and the Suebian. However, following the nation's defeat in the Great Northern War, Skjarnland gradually relinquished its role in European affairs, and is today considered a regional power. Furthermore, the loss of the war in the Northern Front and the ensuing Period of Troubles (1918-1923), and the recent 1934 Skjarnish Political Crisis has significantly eroded overall public trust in the military.

The Armed Forces have undergone significant structural and doctrinal changes since entering the [[great-war|Great War]] in 1914. While the Army was initially successful in its offensives against Karjania from 1914-1915, as the war evolved into static trench warfare following the introduction of Rudanian reinforcements, Skjarnish troops began to suffer high attrition from poor decision-making by the General Staff, at the time still largely populated by aristocrats lacking understanding of modern warfare, and institutional inexperience, with Skjarnland's last major battles being fought in 1815. However, the aftermath of the war allowed for the rise of a more talented class of officers and an urgent reorganization of the military from 1923-28 spearheaded by Anton Stjernberg, including the founding of the Air Force in 1921. Today, while the Skjarnish military is not a perfect meritocracy, organizational restructuring and lessons written in blood during the war have allowed it to become a more formidable and competent fighting force, supplemented by a domestically-grown defense industry that rose as a result of the country's desire to be independent of foreign influence. The Skjarnish Armed Forces' primary mission is home defense and deterrence against foreign aggression, in line with the country's neutral foreign policy.

## History

The Skjarnish Armed Forces traces their lineage back to the Kingdom of Boehus, which was founded in the 10th century, and later unified the Skjarnian states in the decades after the War of the Three Crowns in the late 13th and early 14th centuries, forming the Kingdom of Skjarnia. During this time the Boehusite and Skjarnian militaries distinguished themselves on the battlefield for their superior organisation and steady flow of manpower compared to rival states such as Danyria, despite often being outmatched in individual martial prowess.

During the Skjarnish Wars of Unification (1523-1535), Skjarnian forces effectively utilised pike-and-shot warfare under the leadership of King Johan II and his commanders such as Olof Larsson against Danyrian and Holy Evrian forces, mastering arquebus and pike tactics and popularising the concept of lighter-armoured foot soldiers known as the 'commanded shot' within Northern Evria (kommenderat skjutfolk), demonstrated best during the Battle of Mittjylland in 1532. Following the country's unification in 1535, the Great Power era began, which would see Skjarnish military power reach its zenith.

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/gQvOl4Q.jpeg" alt="'Jiezmene' by Peder Olsen Haugen (1869) depicts Gustaf I inspecting the Cortesian town after the 1631 battle."><figcaption>'Jiezmene' by Peder Olsen Haugen (1869) depicts Gustaf I inspecting the Cortesian town after the 1631 battle.</figcaption></figure>

Skjarnland intervened in the Twenty-Five Years' War on the Veritist side in 1629, thirteen years after its beginning in 1616, in order to protect the continental Veritists and secure the Suebian. Under the leadership of King Gustaf I, Skjarnish forces achieved several major victories against Ecclessiarchial forces in Alemannia and Cortesia, including in the Battles of Jiezmene and Breslach, and revolutionised warfare with the introduction of linear tactics, where units were organised into 6-line-deep formations instead of the 30-line Askurian formation standard during the time. Additionally, Skjarnish forces conquered Livonia following the Siege of Riege and the Dauga Campaign. However, the Battle of Jelgawa in 1635 saw the Skjarnish army suffer a crippling defeat against the Cortesian-led coalition army in the region, losing half of his troops and almost leading to Gustaf's death. Thus, Skjarnland formally withdrew from the war in 1636 after the Peace of Prauvenberg, five years before the continental peace at Lippgau.

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/CTfVBKK.jpeg" alt="The Battle of Visibinsk was the largest battle of the Great Northern War, involving 80,000 Skjarnish and 100,000 Rudanian soldiers fighting over one week in 1707, ultimately ending in a Rudanian retreat."><figcaption>The Battle of Visibinsk was the largest battle of the Great Northern War, involving 80,000 Skjarnish and 100,000 Rudanian soldiers fighting over one week in 1707, ultimately ending in a Rudanian retreat.</figcaption></figure>

Skjarnland continued to assert its influence in continental affairs and defend its position as the dominant power in Northern Evria. This came to a heed with the beginning of the Great Northern War in 1700, which saw a coalition led by Rudania attempt to remove Skjarnland from its Livonian holdings and break its dominance. Despite initial severe defeats by the Rudanians, especially during the First Livonian Campaign (1700-1703) which saw almost 60,000 Rudanians killed for 16,000 Skjarns and no major territorial gains, extreme overextension by King Karl VII's forces during the invasion of Rudania (1706-1713) saw Skjarnland initially defeat Rudanian forces in nearly every major confrontation but run low on supply, turning the tide of the war against them. Rudanian forces adapted quickly, and combined with systematic attacks on Skjarnish logistics and overconfidence by Karl VII, Skjarnish forces were eventually defeated in the Siege of Radagorsk and forced to retreat to Livonia. The Rudanian Navy led a surprise attack on Hävnia in 1713, heavily disrupting Skjarnish maritime logistics and leading to a collapse in morale. Karl VII was killed during the Siege of Riege, and the Treaty of Saulensk saw Skjarnish Livonia formally ceded to Rudania and the end of Skjarnland's role as an Evrian great power.

Skjarnland adopted a policy of neutrality in continental affairs following the Great Northern War. The military then prioritised a defensive stance to protect Skjarnland and her overseas territories. In the late 19th century, however, as tensions rose in Evria, the Armed Forces gradually began rearming. The 1907 annual budget allotted the largest defense budget in Skjarnland's history, with each year until 1918 consistently passing a larger share towards defence. In 1913, Skjarnland solidified its alliance with the Holy Evrian Empire to pursue its nationalist interest of annexing the majority of Karjania, and in August 1914 officially joined the Great War.

Skjarnland's 1st, 2nd, 4th and 5th armies, containing up to 400,000 men, began a large-scale offensive against Karjania, advancing up to 400 km from the border and capturing Murmánska in October 1914 following a joint land offensive and marine naval operation, and Roudanjärvi in January 1915 following a protracted siege. The Karjanian 1915 Winter Counteroffensive failed in its primary goal to liberate Roudanjärvi

but succeeded in forcing Skjarnish forces into a defensive posture and buying time for Rudania, in the process of mobilising, to send the bulk of its reinforcements. From then on, trench warfare on the Northern Front began as both sides prioritised consolidating their gains. In June 1915, Karjanian forces attempted a major offensive in the central front, culminating in the Battle of Syväsjärvi, which stretched until March 1916 and saw 100,000 Skjarnish and 180,000 Karjanian and Rudanian deaths. The Skjarnish Army Air Service, founded in 1908, saw its first major offensive combat deployment in October 1916 with the beginning of the Northern Bombing Campaign, which saw Skjarnish strategic bombers fly sorties against Karjanian and Rudanian cities including Kalarava and Sikov, leading to the largest aerial engagements of the Northern Front.

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/Pb2kADx.png" alt="Skjarnish Marines of the 8th Marine Regiment pictured after landing in Murmánska, October 1914"><figcaption>Skjarnish Marines of the 8th Marine Regiment pictured after landing in Murmánska, October 1914</figcaption></figure>

The Polyakov Offensive of 1917 successfully broke Skjarnish lines in the northern and southern sections, leading to the liberation of Roudanjärvi in July and Murmánska in August. This forced additional mobilisation within Skjarnland and the withdrawal of much of the Skjarnish Expeditionary Force, the contingent sent to the Arverenois Front in 1914 to fight alongside the Holy Evrian Empire, which contained roughly 50,000 men. By September the lines had stabilised once more, although Skjarnland was now purely on the defensive and increasingly reliant on Holy Evrian support. Additionally, losses on the naval front against Elvester and Arverne crippled much of the Skjarnish navy's dreadnoughts, particularly in the 1917 Battle of Jylland. From January to August 1918, Rudanian and Karjanian forces fought a slow, incremental campaign to dislodge Skjarnish forces along the Gustaf Line, ultimately succeeding in forcing Skjarnish forces to withdraw further to the Bergendorff Line. The war ended in armistice in November 1918, leading to a military stalemate, although many observers within Skjarnland consider it a defeat due to the failure of the offensive war's initial objectives.

The end of the Great War saw great political upheaval in Skjarnland, a period known as the 'period of troubles'. In 1919, following a naval mutiny in Malmhug, the Skjarnish military was deployed to restore order and crush further insurrections, such as the Red Soldiers' movement in Strålholm. During this time, the Skjarnish government increased the power of the legislature and expanded voting rights in response to intense political pressure. As such, official investigations into the leadership of the Great War-era military ensued, revealing high levels of unqualifiedness among the highest level of leadership, especially the General Staff, which was dominated by officers from aristocratic lineages. With the public approval of King Gustav IX, several such officers were forcibly retired and mass promotions from the middle and lower officer corps began. In 1923, Anton Stjernberg, who ended the Great War at the rank of Generallöjtnant (Lieutenant General) was promoted to Generalöverste and appointed Minister of Defence in the same year. Stjernberg led urgent military reforms, aiming to turn the officer corps into a complete meritocracy and fundamentally restructure operational and tactical level unit organisation, his reforms concluding in 1928 when he stepped down as Defence Minister and continued to serve under the General Staff.

In April 1934, Communist Party politician Joakim Helgi was arrested on the charges of treason following allegedly threatening remarks against the royal family following the narrow defeat of the Republic Bill, which would have dissolved the Skjarnish monarchy. This caused a political crisis which saw nationwide protests made up of up to 200,000 demonstrators and a general strike enforced by more than 200 unions. In the midst of the upheaval, Chief of the General Staff Hannes Grönwall presented a contingency plan codenamed Odin to King Mikael II to seize critical infrastructure and impose martial law in the most severely affected regions to restore order and government functionality, in fear of foreign threats from the E.S.U. and Karjania. Strikes ended in May 1934, so Odin was never acted upon. However, a leak that month exposed the plan and restored political anger, directed primarily towards the monarchy, resulting in the passing of the New Republic Bill which will formally dissolve Skjarnland's monarchy on 1 January 1935. The crisis has significantly impacted public trust in the military, which will likely again face reorganisation.

## Structure

The King of Skjarnland, Mikael II, is the commander-in-chief of the Armed Forces and theoretically has operational authority. However, since the Great War, in practice, this largely a ceremonial role; actual operational supremacy is centered in the General Staff, currently chaired by General Hannes Grönwall, who reports directly to the King. The Ministry of Defence is responsible for general administration and coordination with the civilian government. Since the stepping down of Anton Stjernberg from the post of Minister of Defence in 1928, Ministry has traditionally been led by a civilian, and reports to the Prime Minister.

### Operational Commands

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:320px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/hEaEO03.jpeg" alt="Skjarnish infantry of the 4th Battalion, 3rd Infantry Division shortly before exercises in Trossö, 1933"><figcaption>Skjarnish infantry of the 4th Battalion, 3rd Infantry Division shortly before exercises in Trossö, 1933</figcaption></figure>

Skjarnland divides its military into five major jurisdictions, known as Operational Commands (Operativ Ledning). The Lappnia, Strålholm and Kjellberg Operational Commands are the three primary homeland defense jurisdictions, while the remaining two, the Arctic and Hyperborean Commands, are overseas jurisdictions whose authority rests primarily inside the Navy.

The Lappnia and Strålholm Commands are primarily concerned with operations on the long border shared with Karjania, and together contain a majority of the Armed Forces' active personnel. The Lappnia Command is equipped for the harsh weather and terrain conditions of northern Skjarnland, and maintaining the Great War-era Bergendorff Line, a fortification network stretching across the Karjanian border. The Strålholm Command's primary mission is the defense of the region around Skjarnland's largest city, and manning the part of the Bergendorff Line on the Strålholmer isthmus (also known as the Strålholmer line). It is also responsible for overseeing naval operations in the upper and middle Suebian sea.

The Kjellberg Command deals with the defense of lower Norrvynia (especially the capital region) and Danyria, and also oversees naval operations in the lower Suebian, the Hlymrströnd (the strait separating Danyria and the rest of Skjarnland) and the North Sea. The well-known Skjarnish Royal Guards, who are responsible for protecting the King are also subordinate to this command since 1933, previously directly answering to the King.

The Arctic Command is primarily responsible for military operations in the Arctic Sea, with additional objectives being the defense of the Spetsbergen Territory and Helluland, maintaining forward bases in both regions. Due to the nature of its mission, operational planning is dominated by the Navy, as with the final jurisdiction, the Hyperborean Command. Despite its name, its main mission is the protection of Skjarnland's other territories outside the mainland and the Arctic rather than being limited to the Hyperborean Ocean.

## Doctrine

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:320px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/YvUWIED.jpeg" alt="Ships of the Suebian Naval Command pictured in Strålholm, 1929"><figcaption>Ships of the Suebian Naval Command pictured in Strålholm, 1929</figcaption></figure>

The Skjarnish Armed Forces have three primary missions as outlined by the Constitution: the defense of Skjarnland against outside threats, the protection of the territorial integrity of Skjarnland, and the aiding of the civilian population in case of emergencies or disasters.

Skjarnish doctrine is primarily defensive, designed to protect its neutrality through deterrence. Against a more powerful enemy force, the 1923 Army Doctrinal Revision emphasizes an organized, mobile retreat into more defensible positions (relying on the country's harsh terrain and weather, especially in the north, and pre-built defensive lines such as the Bergendorff Line) while rearguards conduct a fighting retreat against enemy forces. For example, in case of an enemy invasion of Danyria from the south, the Army is expected to conduct a fighting retreat to Sjaelland and the surrounding islands and sever the bridge connection to the Jylland peninsula while rearguards delay an enemy advance. Army doctrine also emphasizes localized counterattacks meant to disrupt enemy supply lines or inflict heavy casualties in case of an invasion or offensive, thereby buying the main force more time. Further north, the Skjarnish Army relies on the fortified Bergendorff Line, constructed during the Great War, in case of an enemy offensive from Karjania.
