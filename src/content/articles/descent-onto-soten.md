The **Descent onto Sōten** (天孫降臨, *Tenson Kōrin*, “descent of the heavenly grandson”) is the episode of the [[age-of-the-kami]] in which Ninigi, the grandson of the sun kami Amaterasu, came down from heaven with the three imperial regalia to rule the land ceded in the Kuniyuzuri. The *Sōtenki* and the *Nichirin Shoki* set the descent on [[mount-soten|Mount Sōten]]. The court chronology dates it to 2399 BCE, fifty-one sexagenary cycles before the [[raising-of-the-asuka-amenomihashira]].

## Narrative

Amaterasu first chose her son Ame-no-Oshihomimi to rule the land, but he declined in favor of his newborn son Ninigi (瓊瓊杵). Amaterasu gave Ninigi the mirror, the sword Kusanagi, and the seal of the land, and charged him to keep the mirror as he would keep her. Five kami were ordered to descend with him. At Ame-no-Yachimata (天八衢), the crossroads of heaven, the earthly kami Sarutahiko (猿田彦) stood in the procession’s path. When Ame-no-Uzume (天鈿女), one of the five, confronted him, he agreed to guide it down. Ninigi descended onto the summit of Mount Sōten, built his palace there, and later married Konohanasakuya-hime, the kami of the mountain.

## The five attendants

The five attendant kami (五伴緒, *Itsutomonoo*) are said to be the ancestors of the court lineages of the [[mihashira-period|Mihashira period]]:

- Ame-no-Koyane (天児屋) of the court ritualists
- Futodama (太玉) of the keepers of offerings
- Ame-no-Uzume of the hereditary ritual dancers
- Ishikoridome (伊斯許理度売) of the mirror-casters
- Tamanoya (玉祖) of the jewelers

The court ranked these lineages, together with the collateral branches of the imperial house, as the lines of heaven (天神系, *amatsukami-kei*). The clans descended from the earthly kami, which retained the rites of their clan kami under the terms of the [[kuniyuzuri]], it ranked as the lines of the land (国神系, *kunitsukami-kei*). The [[hokyo-code|Hōkyō Code]] reserved the offices of state ritual to the lines of heaven. The royal houses defeated at the Raising of the Asuka Amenomihashira were enrolled among the lines of the land.

## Mount Sōten

Kaiseido holds the summit of Mount Sōten to be the point at which heaven met the land, and the ascent of the mountain has been a pilgrimage since the Mihashira period. The imperial court recorded eruptions of the volcano as judgments of heaven on the reigning sovereign. Critics of the [[nichirin|shogunate]] read the eruption of 1803, which caused the [[famine-of-the-falling-heaven]], as a judgment on the Miyamoto clan.
